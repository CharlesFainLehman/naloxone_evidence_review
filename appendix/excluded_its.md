# Appendix: studies considered but excluded from the main analyses

These studies estimate an association between naloxone availability and overdose mortality but were excluded from the main figures under the control rule used here. For interrupted-time-series (ITS), controlled ITS, and other panel designs, the main analysis requires unit fixed effects or an equivalent design-based strategy that controls for stable differences across units, and a naloxone effect that is separately identified. This appendix is not a claim that the studies are uninformative; it records why they were not pooled with the quasi-experimental estimates. The underlying table is `data/excluded_its.csv`.

Studies are listed by year. The first column of the table gives the reason class: **no unit FE** (multi-unit panel without unit fixed effects), **one-group** (single series with no comparison unit), **bundled** (naloxone effect not separable from another policy), or **allocation** (authors interpret the association as targeting rather than effect).

## Notes

- **Walley et al. (2013):** the paper calls the design an interrupted time series and compares implementation intensity across community-year strata. Its methods describe Poisson regression with measured community covariates and an autoregressive covariance structure rather than community fixed effects.
- **Naumann et al. (2019)** and **Xuan et al. (2024)** both provide nonimplementing units as a comparison series, but both estimate population-average effects with GEE and no unit fixed effects.
- **Hamilton et al. (2021)** is the only county-level national panel in this group. It would move to the main analysis if the authors reported an NAL-only estimate.
- **Taylor et al. (2022):** the interruption is the 2017 Maryland standing-order expansion; there is no untreated jurisdiction. The full text has not been checked.
- **Toce et al. (2024):** the analysis is a population-averaged GEE, not a state-fixed-effects panel model (confirmed in the supplement). The commonly quoted IRRs are trend ratios, not level changes.
- **Newman et al. (2025):** this is a genuine controlled ITS, but the control is a comparison of three regional time series.
- **Morgan et al. (2022)** and **Walley et al. (2026)** are dose-response analyses of distribution volume rather than evaluations of a discrete policy change; both find no protective association at the community level.

Zang et al. (2023), an interrupted time-series analysis centered on the onset of COVID-19, is not listed here because its interruption is the pandemic rather than a naloxone intervention. It was excluded for the estimand, not for the ITS control issue.

Studies checked and set aside as descriptive, uncontrolled single-site pre/post comparisons, individual-level cohorts, or simulation models (not listed above): Rando 2015, Rowe 2016, Freiermuth 2023, Brown 2024, You 2020, Seo 2026, Ghobadi 2024, Cloutier 2023, Parmar 2017 (N-ALIVE, stopped early with no effect estimate), Cai 2024/2025 (theoretical model), Irvine 2019/2026, Zang 2024/2025, Chhatwal 2026, Tatara 2024.
