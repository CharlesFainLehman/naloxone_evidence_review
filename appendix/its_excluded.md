# Appendix: interrupted time-series studies excluded from the primary analysis

The primary figures exclude interrupted time-series (ITS) and controlled interrupted time-series (CITS) studies that did not meet the working control standard used for the main analysis. In practice, these studies lacked unit fixed effects or an equivalently strong design feature for controlling persistent cross-sectional differences.

| Study | Setting / period | Design and controls | Mortality result | Why excluded from primary analysis | Link |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Walley et al. 2013 | 19 Massachusetts communities, 2002–2009 | Interrupted time series using community-year observations; Poisson regression adjusted for observed community covariates and an AR(1) covariance structure; concurrent no/low/high OEND implementation strata, but no community fixed effects | ARR 0.73 (95% CI 0.57–0.91) for 1–100 OEND enrollments/100k; ARR 0.54 (0.39–0.76) for >100/100k, versus no implementation | Geographic comparisons remain vulnerable to time-invariant differences across communities because the model does not absorb community-specific fixed effects | [BMJ](https://www.bmj.com/content/346/bmj.f174) |
| Taylor et al. 2022 | Maryland, 2015–2019 | Single-state interrupted time series plus multivariable Poisson regression; no untreated comparison jurisdiction and no unit fixed effects | After the 2017 expanded standing order, reported declines were 0.25 prescription-opioid deaths/100k, 1.83 heroin deaths/100k, and 2.54 fentanyl deaths/100k | One-group pre/post trend break cannot separate the policy from coincident statewide shocks or changes in the drug supply | [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35467459/) |
| Toce et al. 2024 | U.S. states, youths age 15–24, 1999–2021 | Population-averaged negative-binomial interrupted time-series model by type of naloxone access law; no state fixed effects meeting the primary-analysis threshold | Review characterized the association as null. Deaths rose 7.8%/year pre-NAL; post-implementation slopes were +4.7%/year for patient-specific NALs and +8.7%/year for patient-nonspecific NALs | Population-averaged ITS does not absorb persistent state-specific determinants of overdose mortality | [JAMA Pediatrics](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapediatrics/fullarticle/2824897) |
| Xuan et al. 2024 | 351 Massachusetts municipalities, 2013–2018 | Multisite ITS using segmented Poisson regression with GEE and measured municipal covariates; municipalities without standing-order dispensing served as a comparison group, but the model does not include municipality fixed effects | Annualized slope RR 0.84 (95% CI 0.78–0.91) after standing-order naloxone dispensing, relative to nonimplementing municipalities | Stronger than an uncontrolled ITS, but still excluded because persistent municipality-level differences are not absorbed by fixed effects | [JAMA Network Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2822958) |
| Newman et al. 2025 | St. Louis City, St. Louis County, and three collar counties, 2011–2022 | Controlled interrupted time series using autoregressive ARIMA models; separate regional series and interaction comparisons, but no area fixed-effects panel specification | In St. Louis City, naloxone distribution was associated with an immediate −2.17 deaths/100k/month level change (95% CI −3.28 to −1.06) and a −0.28/month slope change (−0.37 to −0.18); effects were not replicated in comparison areas | CITS provides contemporaneous regional comparisons, but the model does not use unit fixed effects and the treatment effect is identified from segmented regional trends | [Journal of Substance Use & Addiction Treatment](https://www.jsatjournal.com/article/S2949-8759%2824%2900276-5/fulltext) |

## Scope note

Zang et al. (2023), an interrupted time-series study of overdose deaths and naloxone distribution around the onset of COVID-19, is not included in this appendix because its interruption is the pandemic rather than a naloxone intervention. It was therefore excluded for a different reason: it does not estimate the causal effect of naloxone access on mortality.
