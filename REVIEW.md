# Review of the naloxone evidence table

Scope: every row in `data/effects.csv` and `data/excluded_its.csv` was checked against the source paper (full text where obtainable, otherwise abstract), the Khezri et al. (2026) review was cross-checked for omissions, and a broad search was run for quasi-experimental naloxone-mortality studies outside the review (economics working papers, public-health journals, international programs, 2024–2026 output). Code and figures were checked for internal consistency.

Full text was obtained for: Rees (NBER WP version), McClellan, Atkins, Erfanian, Cataife, Abouk, Doleac & Mukherjee (published JLE version), HEALing Communities, Freisthler, Payne, Lee (plus replication code), Peet, Walley 2013, Toce, Xuan, Newman, Zang. Abstract only (publisher sites blocked from this environment): Dowd, Duska, Sohn, Taylor, and the published JLE tables of Rees.

## 1. Headline findings

1. **Nine of twelve relative-effect rows were not taken from the papers.** Rees, McClellan, Atkins, Erfanian, and Doleac & Mukherjee reproduce, digit for digit, the ratio entries in Table 3 of Smart, Pardo & Davis (2021, *Addiction*). Abouk and Sohn are conversions from absolute effects. Dowd's percentages are exp(β)−1 conversions of log coefficients. `data/effects.csv` labels all of these "As reported"; none of the papers prints those percentages or CIs.
2. **Erfanian et al. (2019) is misrepresented.** The row shows the within-state direct effect only (0.238 deaths/100k, p = 0.55, converted to +3% of the sample mean). The paper's headline is a large, significant positive spillover effect on neighboring states (indirect effect 5.767 deaths/100k, p < 0.001) and a conclusion that NALs do not reduce deaths in the adopting state. The CI (−11%, +18%) cannot be derived from the paper's reported p-value, which implies roughly (−8%, +15%). Author order is also wrong: Erfanian, Collins, Grossman.
3. **McClellan's CI is not in the paper.** The paper reports "14% lower incidence (p = 0.033)" and a figure. The repo's (−22%, −1%) is Smart's reading of the figure. A CI implied by the p-value is about (−25%, −1%). The model uses state random effects, not fixed effects, with a one-year treatment lag and no policy covariates.
4. **Two rows are co-prescribing mandate studies, not naloxone access or distribution studies.** Sohn et al. (2023) and Duska et al. (2022) both evaluate state naloxone co-prescribing mandates. Neither row says so. Sohn's −16% applies only to prescription/treatment-opioid deaths; the illicit/synthetic estimate is null (+0.32 deaths per state-quarter, 95% CI −18.27 to 18.91).
5. **Payne (2026) is not an access-law study.** It evaluates an Indiana grant program distributing naloxone kits to county health departments (institutional supply to first responders), using TWFE on 92 county-years 2014–2019. The README's selection step 4 groups it with NAL papers. The Callaway–Sant'Anna robustness estimate is 1.43 (SE 1.66), so the sign is not estimator-driven.
6. **Peet et al. outcome and period are mislabeled.** The plotted outcome is *non-synthetic* opioid age-adjusted mortality; synthetic-opioid effects (Table 4) are null. Experiment (2) uses a 2010–2019 sample, not 2016–2019 as the README states. Experiment (2) is a Narcan-introduction effect in states with existing NALs, not an NAL-adoption effect.
7. **Lee et al. outcome is all-drug overdose deaths**, not opioid deaths. The 1344.3 figure is a REML random-effects pooled mean of thirteen quarterly lead-specific ATTs (leads 0–12), so the annualized number is an average over the first three post-adoption years. The /3000 and ×4 conversions are correct (verified against the replication code).
8. **Doleac & Mukherjee note is wrong.** The README says the numeric estimate comes from the working-paper version summarized by Smart. The published JLE Table 4 contains the same coefficient (0.006, SE 0.027, baseline 0.601 deaths/100k/month), which converts to +1.0% (−7.8%, +9.8%). Smart's rounding gives the repo's (−8, +10).
9. **The figures were not produced by the R script.** Both SVGs are hand-written (explicit `<circle>` and `<line>` elements; R's svg device emits paths and glyph definitions). Their plotted positions do match `data/effects.csv` exactly. The README says the script writes PNGs and `data/effects_reproduced.csv`; neither is committed. `figures/STYLE_NOTE.txt` refers to PNGs that do not exist. R is not installed in this environment, so the script was not run.
10. **Missing studies.** No experimental or quasi-experimental study in Khezri or Smart is absent from the repo. Outside those reviews, one county-level NAL mortality study (Rudolph et al. 2022) and one panel-fixed-effects distribution study (Spackman et al. 2025) plausibly meet the criteria, and several ITS/GEE studies belong in the appendix for consistency with Walley and Xuan. Details in section 5.

## 4. Provenance and labeling

- `data/effects.csv` `scaling` column reads "As reported" for every relative row. Correct labels: Rees, Atkins, Cataife, Dowd = converted from log coefficients; McClellan = point from text, CI read from figure (via Smart); Erfanian = direct effect divided by sample mean, CI untraceable; Abouk, Sohn = converted from absolute effect and baseline mean; Doleac & Mukherjee = converted from level coefficient and baseline. Only HCS and Freisthler report a ratio directly.
- Design labels "Controlled pre-post" (Rees, Atkins) are Smart's terminology, not the authors'. Both papers are TWFE Poisson models.
- Outcome definitions differ across rows and are not recorded: opioid-related (multiple-cause T-codes, Rees); opioid overdose (underlying-cause poisoning plus T-codes, McClellan, Atkins, Abouk); all-drug overdose (Lee, Freisthler); non-synthetic opioid (Peet); prescription/treatment opioid (Sohn); opioid-related per author coding (Payne).
- README study table has two columns headed "Study."
- `data/effects.csv` carries floating-point noise (e.g., −0.6239999999999997).
- `geom_errorbarh` is deprecated in ggplot2 ≥ 3.5; the script will warn.

### 5b. Appendix candidates (ITS/GEE without unit FE, same class as Walley 2013 and Xuan 2024)

- Naumann et al. 2019, *Drug Alcohol Depend* 204:107536: North Carolina counties, GEE with AR(1), kits per 100k; RR 0.90 (0.78–1.04) for 1–100 kits and 0.88 (0.76–1.02) for >100 kits. In Khezri; the closest analog to Walley and the most obvious omission.
- Antoniou et al. 2022, *Drug Alcohol Depend* 236:109473: Ontario pharmacy naloxone program, single-province ITS 2012–2018; level RR 0.91 (0.79–1.06).
- Yeung et al. 2023, *CMAJ Open* 11(3):E537: Alberta municipal ITS; community naloxone program associated with a +9.1 deaths/month urban level increase, coincident with fentanyl arrival.
- Allen et al. 2022, *Ann Med* 54:2692: Clark County NV vending machines, single-series ARIMA; −8.52 deaths/month (p = 0.002).
- Goodman et al. 2026, medRxiv 2026.04.24.26351679: Galveston County, uncontrolled weekly ITS; post-intervention trend IRR 0.9945/week (0.990–0.999).
- Håkansson et al. 2024, *BMJ Open* 14:e074152: Skåne, Sweden, 2019–2021 vs 2013–2017 historic control; IRR 0.72 (0.56–0.92). No comparison region.
- Bird et al. 2016, *Addiction* 111:883: Scotland national programme, uncontrolled pre/post; share of opioid deaths within 4 weeks of prison release fell 36% (95% CI 20–51%).

### 5c. Checked and do not meet

Seo et al. 2026 (Indianapolis, bundled intervention, no inferential mortality estimate); Ghobadi et al. 2024 (California co-prescription mandate, Kaiser ED cohort, 30-day mortality DID −0.01, −0.03 to 0.01); Cloutier et al. 2023 (Pennsylvania county coordination, naloxone is a covariate); Cai 2024/2025 (SSRN 4921020, 5797302: theoretical compartmental model); Rando 2015, Freiermuth 2023, Brown 2024, You 2020 (descriptive or uncontrolled pre/post); Parmar et al. 2017 N-ALIVE RCT (stopped early, no effect estimate); Irvine 2019/2026, Zang 2024/2025, Chhatwal 2026, Tatara 2024 (modeling); Lawlor 2026 (Carleton undergraduate thesis on OTC naloxone, state-month DID, full text request-only).

No peer-reviewed or working-paper evaluation of the 2023 OTC Narcan switch on mortality exists as of September 2026. No new NAL paper using heterogeneity-robust staggered estimators was found beyond Peet (2024) and Payne (2026). Gilbert et al. 2025 (*Int J Drug Policy* 140:104798) is the HCS nonfatal-overdose secondary analysis (aRR 0.85, 0.74–0.96) and is the natural companion to the two HCS rows.

Two items could not be resolved: Freisthler et al., "Effects of a Naloxone 'Surges' in Ohio on Opioid Overdose Deaths," *Drug Alcohol Depend* 267:111868 (2025; almost certainly a CPDD meeting abstract), and Jun & Xue, "From Overdose Prevention to Reproductive Health: The Impact of Naloxone Access Laws," SSRN 5589316 (2025; primary outcome is not mortality).
